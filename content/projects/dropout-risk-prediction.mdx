## Overview

A system that predicts which students are at risk of dropping out of university, so that support can reach them early. It was my final-year project. I trained and compared four models on primary survey data from southwest Nigerian universities and deployed the result as a Flask web app.

## Problem

The goal was to estimate a student's dropout risk early enough to act on it, using data collected from the students' own context in southwest Nigeria rather than a foreign dataset.

## My role

I ran the whole project: designing the approach with CRISP-DM, working with the survey data, building and comparing the models, and deploying the Flask application.

## Architecture

The pipeline follows CRISP-DM:

1. **Data:** primary survey responses from southwest Nigerian universities.
2. **Preparation:** cleaning and encoding, then augmentation to enlarge the training data.
3. **Modelling:** Logistic Regression, Random Forest, XGBoost, and a soft-voting ensemble of them.
4. **Evaluation:** accuracy and recall on each model.
5. **Deployment:** a Flask web app that takes a student's answers and returns a risk prediction.

## Key decisions & trade-offs

- **Primary data over a public dataset.** The survey reflects the students and institutions the system is meant for. The trade-off is a smaller sample than a public dataset, which is why augmentation was used.
- **Recall as a headline metric.** Missing a student who is genuinely at risk is the costly error, so recall matters alongside accuracy.
- **Four models compared, including an ensemble.** Starting from an interpretable baseline (Logistic Regression) and adding Random Forest, XGBoost and a soft-voting ensemble made the comparison honest.
- **Flask for deployment.** It is lightweight and enough to turn a trained model into a usable web tool.

## Results

On augmented data, the Random Forest reached **94.1% accuracy and 98.0% recall**.
